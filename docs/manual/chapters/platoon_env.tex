\chapter{Vehicle Platoon Control}\label{chap:platoon}

\code{env\_lib.platoon\_env.PlatoonEnv} (registered as \envid{Platoon-v0}, new in
version 1.1) is a benchmark for cooperative adaptive cruise control (CACC). A
leader vehicle is followed by $n$ follower vehicles on a single lane. The
followers are the agents: each commands its own acceleration and must keep a
speed-dependent distance to its predecessor while the leader drives an
exogenous speed profile with cruising phases, stop-and-go waves and random
speed changes. The vehicles have heterogeneous drivetrain lags and exchange data
over a vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) communication topology, which determines what
each agent observes.

The environment reproduces the central phenomenon of platoon control,
\emph{string stability}: with sensor-only adaptive cruise control (ACC) and a
short time headway, spacing errors grow from vehicle to vehicle along the
platoon until vehicles collide, while cooperative ACC, which also uses the
acceleration communicated by the predecessor, attenuates them.
Section~\ref{sec:platoon-baseline} demonstrates this numerically. Typical
research uses are learning decentralised controllers that are safe, comfortable
and string stable, comparing communication topologies, and studying robustness
to heterogeneous vehicles. The dynamics are written once for a batch of
platoons, so \code{PlatoonVectorEnv} simulates thousands of platoons at once
(Section~\ref{sec:platoon-vector}).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Model}\label{sec:platoon-model}

\paragraph{Vehicles.} Vehicle $0$ is the leader and vehicles $i = 1, \dots, n$
are the followers (agent index $k = i - 1$ in all arrays). Vehicle $i$ has the
position $p_i$ of its front bumper, speed $v_i$, acceleration $a_i$, length
$L_i$ and a first-order actuator (drivetrain) lag with time constant $\tau_i$:
\begin{equation}
  \dot p_i = v_i, \qquad \dot v_i = a_i, \qquad
  \dot a_i = \frac{u_i - a_i}{\tau_i},
  \label{eq:platoon-dynamics}
\end{equation}
where $u_i$ is the commanded acceleration, the action of follower $i$, clipped
to $[a_{\min}, a_{\max}]$. The command is held constant over a step of length
$\Delta t$ and \eqref{eq:platoon-dynamics} is discretised exactly (zero-order
hold). With $\alpha_i = e^{-\Delta t/\tau_i}$ and
$c_i = \tau_i \Delta t - \tau_i^2 (1 - \alpha_i)$,
\begin{align}
  a_i^{+} &= \alpha_i a_i + (1 - \alpha_i)\, u_i, \\
  v_i^{+} &= v_i + \tau_i (1 - \alpha_i)\, a_i
             + \bigl(\Delta t - \tau_i (1 - \alpha_i)\bigr)\, u_i, \\
  p_i^{+} &= p_i + \Delta t\, v_i + c_i\, a_i + \bigl(\tfrac12 \Delta t^2 - c_i\bigr)\, u_i .
\end{align}
The update is vectorised over vehicles and platoons. Vehicles do not reverse: a
vehicle whose speed would become negative within a step is held at standstill
($v = 0$, $a = \max(a, 0)$, position not decreased); the position error of this
approximation is below $v \Delta t / 2$ at the moment of stopping. The lags and
lengths are drawn uniformly from \code{tau\_range} and \code{length\_range} with a
generator seeded by \code{vehicle\_seed}, so every episode uses the same
heterogeneous platoon, or from \code{self.np\_random} at every reset when
\code{randomize\_vehicles=True}.

\paragraph{Spacing policy.} Follower $i$ keeps a constant \emph{time headway}
$h$ to its predecessor. With the gap $g_i$ (rear bumper of the predecessor to
front bumper of follower $i$), the standstill distance $r$ and the desired gap
$d_i^{*}$,
\begin{equation}
  g_i = p_{i-1} - p_i - L_{i-1}, \qquad d_i^{*} = r + h\, v_i, \qquad
  e_i = g_i - d_i^{*}, \qquad \Delta v_i = v_{i-1} - v_i,
  \label{eq:platoon-spacing}
\end{equation}
where $e_i$ is the spacing error and $\Delta v_i$ the relative speed. Note that
$\dot e_i = \Delta v_i - h\, a_i$: a platoon that tracks the spacing policy
exactly has $\Delta v_i = h\, a_i \neq 0$ while it accelerates or brakes.

\paragraph{Leader.} The leader has the same dynamics; its command follows a
speed profile drawn per episode from \code{self.np\_random}. The profile starts
at the initial cruise speed $v_c \sim U[16, 25]$\,m/s, which is held for
2--5\,s, and continues with a sequence of events. Every event has two segments,
each of which ramps the reference speed to a target with constant acceleration
and then holds it (Table~\ref{tab:platoon-scenarios}); segment boundaries are
aligned with the time grid, and after the last event the speed is held. The
commands are stored per step ($8 \times$ \code{max\_steps} bytes per platoon).
Because of the lag, the leader's speed approaches every target from one side,
without overshoot.

\code{scenario="cruise"}, \code{"stop\_and\_go"} and \code{"random"} use one event
type throughout. The default \code{"mixed"} starts with a stop-and-go wave and
draws every further event uniformly from the three types, so that every episode
contains a hard braking manoeuvre in which the string instability of naive
controllers becomes visible.

\paragraph{Initial state.} The platoon starts near equilibrium at $v_c$: zero
accelerations, the leader at $p_0 = 0$, follower speeds $v_c + \sigma_v \xi_i$
and gaps $r + h v_c + \sigma_g \xi'_i$ with standard normal $\xi_i, \xi'_i$
truncated at three standard deviations ($\sigma_v$ =
\code{init\_speed\_noise}, $\sigma_g$ = \code{init\_spacing\_noise}; gaps are
kept above half the desired gap).
\code{reset(options=...)} overrides parts of the episode; all arrays list the
leader first:
\code{"positions"} and \code{"velocities"} (shape \code{(n+1,)}, given
together), \code{"accelerations"} (default zeros), \code{"leader\_command"}
(shape \code{(max\_steps,)}, commanded accelerations of the leader, clipped to
the action bounds), \code{"time\_constants"} and \code{"lengths"} (this episode
only). Values of these keys are validated strictly; unknown keys are ignored with
a \code{UserWarning} (PettingZoo's API test passes a dummy option).

\section{Spaces}\label{sec:platoon-spaces}

\paragraph{Action.} \code{Box(accel\_min, accel\_max, (n,), float32)}, the
commanded accelerations in m/s$^2$ (default $[-6, 3]$). Any array with $n$
entries is accepted; it is cast to \code{float32} like the action space and
clipped to the bounds; NaN or infinite values raise \code{ValueError}.

\paragraph{Observation.} An unbounded \code{Box} of shape \code{(n, 13)},
\code{float32}; row $k$ belongs to follower $i = k + 1$
(Table~\ref{tab:platoon-obs}). Every feature is a physical quantity divided by
a fixed scale (\code{FEATURE\_SCALES}). Radar measurements and the own state
are always present. Communicated channels that the topology does not provide
are zero with availability flag 0, so the observation size does not depend on
the topology. \code{env.observation\_layout} maps every feature name
(\code{OBSERVATION\_FEATURES}) to its column slice.

\section{Reward and episode end}\label{sec:platoon-reward}

Follower $i$ receives, after the step,
\begin{equation}
  r_i = -\bigl(w_e e_i^2 + w_v \Delta v_i^2 + w_u u_i^2 + w_j j_i^2\bigr)\,\Delta t
        \;-\; P_c\, [g_i \le 0] \;-\; P_b\, [g_i > g_{\max}],
  \label{eq:platoon-reward}
\end{equation}
with the clipped command $u_i$, the jerk $j_i = (a_i^{+} - a_i)/\Delta t$, the
weights $w_e$ = \code{spacing\_weight} (1.0), $w_v$ = \code{speed\_weight} (0.1),
$w_u$ = \code{control\_weight} (0.02) and $w_j$ = \code{jerk\_weight} (0.01), the
collision penalty $P_c$ = \code{collision\_penalty} (500), the break-up penalty
$P_b$ = \code{breakup\_penalty} (250) and the break-up distance $g_{\max}$ =
\code{breakup\_distance} (100\,m). The team reward returned by \code{step()} is
$R = \sum_i r_i$.

The weights make tracking dominate while keeping comfort relevant. The
relative-speed weight is small because $\Delta v_i \ne 0$ is inherent to the
time-headway policy during manoeuvres (Section~\ref{sec:platoon-model}); the
control and jerk terms mainly penalise aggressive or oscillating commands. Over
20 episodes of the default scenario, the four terms make up 5, 60, 33 and
2\,\% of the cost of the CACC baseline (which tracks almost perfectly) and 65,
31, 4 and 0.2\,\% of the cost of ACC with $h = 1.2$\,s, whose cost is eight
times higher. The collision penalty exceeds the cost of a whole ACC episode of
the entire team (about 400), so for controllers of that quality ending an
episode early by a collision does not pay off.

\code{terminated} is \code{True} after a collision ($g_i \le 0$) or a break-up
($g_i > g_{\max}$) of any follower; \code{truncated} is \code{True} after
\code{max\_steps} steps.

The defaults were chosen as follows. A headway of 0.6\,s is a typical CACC
setting and short enough that sensor-only ACC is string unstable. Lags of
0.2--0.4\,s are heterogeneous yet within the range for which the CACC
baseline is string stable (up to 0.43\,s, Section~\ref{sec:platoon-baseline}).
The command bounds allow emergency braking at twice the leader's hardest
braking (4\,m/s$^2$). The initial perturbations are small compared with the
errors caused by the leader, so that the peak-error statistics measure the
propagation of disturbances rather than the initial condition.

\section{Baseline controllers and string stability}\label{sec:platoon-baseline}

\code{cacc\_policy(obs, *, k\_p=0.5, k\_d=0.6, k\_a=0.65, accel\_min=-6.0,
accel\_max=3.0)} is the decentralised linear CACC law
\begin{equation}
  u_i = k_p\, e_i + k_d\, (v_{i-1} - v_i) + k_a\, a_{i-1},
  \label{eq:platoon-cacc}
\end{equation}
a pure function of each follower's own observation row that works for any
leading batch shape, \code{(..., n, 13)} $\to$ \code{(..., n)}. The predecessor
acceleration $a_{i-1}$ is taken from the V2V channel and multiplied by the
availability flag, so without communication (\code{topology="none"}) and for
\code{k\_a=0} the law is sensor-only ACC. \code{env\_lib.baseline\_policy(env)}
binds it with the action bounds of the environment.

\paragraph{Choice of the gains.} The law has the structure of the CACC designs
studied in the platooning literature: a PD feedback on the spacing error and
the relative speed plus a feed-forward of the acceleration that the predecessor
transmits over V2V. That literature shows that the feed-forward is what makes
string stability possible at much shorter time headways than with ACC. For
identical vehicles and the exact discretisation above, the commands of
consecutive followers satisfy $U_i(z) = \Gamma(z)\, U_{i-1}(z)$ with
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma = \frac{k_p H_p + k_d H_v + k_a H_a}{1 + k_p H_p + (k_p h + k_d) H_v},
  \label{eq:platoon-string}
\end{equation}
where $H_p$, $H_v$ and $H_a$ are the transfer functions from the command to the
position, speed and acceleration of one vehicle; the same ratio relates
consecutive positions, speeds and accelerations. The platoon is string stable
when $\max_\omega |\Gamma(e^{j\omega\Delta t})| \le 1$; since $\Gamma(1) = 1$ the
peak is never below one. \code{string\_stability\_gain(k\_p, k\_d, k\_a, *,
headway, time\_constant, dt)} evaluates this peak (and returns \code{inf} if the
vehicle's own loop is unstable). The gains were chosen with it: for
$k_p = 0.5$\,s$^{-2}$ and $k_d = 0.6$\,s$^{-1}$ the peak equals one for all lags
in $[0.2, 0.4]$\,s at $h = 0.6$\,s exactly when $k_a \in [0.575, 0.755]$, and
$k_a = 0.65$ lies in the middle of this interval. A full feed-forward
($k_a = 1$) is not string stable with a lagged drivetrain (peak 1.25), because
the measured acceleration of the predecessor lags its command.
Table~\ref{tab:platoon-gain} lists the peak over the lag range for both
controllers: CACC is string stable from $h \approx 0.56$\,s on, ACC with the
same feedback gains only from $h \approx 1.14$\,s on; with the default gains
CACC stays string stable at $h = 0.6$\,s for lags up to 0.43\,s.

\paragraph{Numerical demonstration.} Table~\ref{tab:platoon-baselines}
compares random actions, ACC at the default headway, ACC at $h = 1.2$\,s and
CACC in 20 episodes per scenario (reset seeds 0--19, 8 followers, predecessor
following, default parameters otherwise). ACC at $h = 0.6$\,s is string unstable:
in the cruise scenario the peak spacing error grows from the first to the last
follower by a factor of 2.4 (median), and in every stop-and-go episode the
amplified braking wave ends in a collision after about 11\,s. ACC with the
longer headway of 1.2\,s is string stable again (amplification below one) but
keeps twice the distance and tracks worse. CACC at $h = 0.6$\,s never collides,
attenuates the errors along the platoon (median last-to-first ratio 0.35) and
keeps them below 0.42\,m in all stop-and-go episodes. It clearly beats random
actions, which collide within about 7\,s.

\section{Vector environment}\label{sec:platoon-vector}

\code{PlatoonVectorEnv(num\_envs, *, autoreset\_mode="next\_step",
render\_mode=None, **kwargs)} takes the keyword arguments of \code{PlatoonEnv}
and simulates \code{num\_envs} platoons with the same batched core, so a copy
evolves exactly like a single environment started from the same state (the
test suite checks bit-for-bit equality of copy 0 with a single environment).
\code{env\_lib.make\_vec("Platoon-v0", num\_envs)} creates it. Observations have
shape \code{(num\_envs, n, 13)}, actions \code{(num\_envs, n)}, rewards are the team
rewards of the copies and the info arrays gain a leading \code{num\_envs}
dimension (\code{agent\_rewards} has shape \code{(num\_envs, n)}). Reset options may
have the single-environment shape (applied to every reset copy) or an extra
leading \code{num\_envs} dimension. With the default \code{"next\_step"}
autoreset, the info rows of the copies reset in a step describe their new
initial state, as in \code{gymnasium.vector.SyncVectorEnv}. \code{render()}
draws copy 0. All copies share one generator, so copy $b$ does not reproduce a
single environment seeded with \code{seed + b}.

Table~\ref{tab:platoon-throughput} reports the measured throughput of
\code{step()} (CACC actions, no resets during the measurement; policy time
excluded) against \code{gymnasium.vector.SyncVectorEnv} of the single
environment, on a 4-core x86-64 machine with Python 3.11 and NumPy 2.4 (one
thread). A single \code{PlatoonEnv.step()} takes about 78\,$\mu$s for 8 as for
64 followers. With random actions, which end episodes after about 65 steps and
therefore reset many copies every step, the native environment reaches
0.65\,M environment steps per second for $n = 8$ and $B = 1024$ and
0.13\,M for $n = 64$.

\section{Rendering}\label{sec:platoon-render}

The dashboard ($1000 \times 560$ pixels, Figure~\ref{fig:platoon}) shows the
road at the top: a two-lane strip seen from above with a camera that follows the
platoon (moving lane markings and distance posts). Vehicles are rounded
rectangles drawn to scale in length; the leader is drawn in the text colour and
labelled, the followers in a sequential colour ramp from the first to the last
follower. Brake lights glow when a vehicle decelerates. The gap in front of
every follower is shaded by its spacing error (red: too close, blue: too far,
more opaque for larger errors), a tick marks the desired position, and the V2V
links of the topology are drawn as arcs with moving data packets. Below, the
spacing errors of all followers over time show how disturbances propagate, the
speed panel shows the leader, its reference profile for the whole episode and
the followers, and the bar chart gives the peak spacing error of every follower
together with the last-to-first ratio. The header reports the team size, the
topology, step, time, leader speed and minimum gap, with a status badge
(CRUISING, COLLISION, BREAK-UP or COMPLETE). Both themes are supported. An
\code{"rgb\_array"} frame takes about 24\,ms for 8 followers and 40\,ms for 64
(mean over an episode; the first frame, which builds the figure, about 0.25\,s).

\section{Usage}\label{sec:platoon-usage}

\begin{pycode}
import env_lib
from env_lib.platoon_env import cacc_policy

env = env_lib.make("Platoon-v0", topology="predecessor_leader",
                   scenario="stop_and_go")
obs, info = env.reset(seed=0)                  # obs: (8, 13) float32
layout = env.unwrapped.observation_layout
print(obs[:, layout["leader_available"]].ravel())  # all ones for PLF
episode_return = 0.0
while True:
    action = cacc_policy(obs)                  # ACC: cacc_policy(obs, k_a=0.0)
    obs, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)
    episode_return += reward
    if terminated or truncated:
        break
print(info["collision"], info["peak_spacing_errors"], episode_return)
env.close()

# 1024 platoons in one batch; the same policy works on the batched observation.
envs = env_lib.make_vec("Platoon-v0", num_envs=1024)
obs, infos = envs.reset(seed=0)                # obs: (1024, 8, 13)
for _ in range(600):
    obs, rewards, terminated, truncated, infos = envs.step(cacc_policy(obs))
envs.close()
\end{pycode}

A custom leader profile is passed through the reset options, for example a
constant speed with \code{env.reset(options=\{"leader\_command":
np.zeros(env.unwrapped.max\_steps)\})}; with \code{init\_spacing\_noise=0} and
\code{init\_speed\_noise=0} the platoon then starts and stays in equilibrium
under CACC.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:platoon-repro}

The leader profile, the initial perturbations and, with
\code{randomize\_vehicles=True}, the vehicle parameters are drawn from
\code{self.np\_random}, seeded by \code{reset(seed=...)}. With the default
\code{randomize\_vehicles=False} the heterogeneous platoon is fixed by
\code{vehicle\_seed} and identical in every instance, including every copy of a
vector environment. The simulation itself is deterministic.
